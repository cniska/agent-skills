# Usability heuristics

Nielsen's ten usability heuristics, each a heading a finding names as its **Rule**, with what it asks of a screen.

## Visibility of system status

The screen shows what is happening now, such as a save in progress or a sync that failed, soon enough to act on.

## Match between system and the real world

Words, order and icons are the user's own, not the code's or the database's.

## User control and freedom

Every step has a clear exit and an undo, so a mistaken tap costs nothing.

## Consistency and standards

The same thing looks, reads and behaves the same everywhere, and the platform's conventions hold.

## Error prevention

The design makes the slip impossible or confirms before it, ahead of any error message.

## Recognition rather than recall

What the user needs to choose is on screen, not something they must remember from a step before.

## Flexibility and efficiency of use

Frequent users get shortcuts that leave the plain path intact for new ones.

## Aesthetic and minimalist design

Each element earns its place; what is rarely needed does not compete with what is.

## Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors

An error says in plain words what went wrong and how to fix it.

## Help and documentation

Help, where a task needs it, is short, findable and about the task at hand.
