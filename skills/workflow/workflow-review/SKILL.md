# Workflow review

Find where the workflow keeps failing the owner, and propose what should change so it stops. A single slip shows little, while a pattern across weeks of work shows a skill is wrong. The usual fix is a shorter skill.

## What matters

- **Look at a long window**: at least four weeks, or since the skill last changed shape, whichever is longer. One day shows incidents, not patterns.
- **A pattern needs several independent sessions.** List a one-off without a proposal.
- **The owner's correction is the evidence.** When an agent followed a skill and the owner corrected it, the skill is wrong. Restate what the owner said as a plain statement in your own words, and cite it by session and time.
- **Judge the whole skill, not the line.** Read it end to end: does it state its purpose, can an agent follow it in one read, and did it grow by patches? Its history over the window shows patches as many small additions after slips. A skill that grew this way gets a rewrite from its purpose, never another clause.
- **Cut before adding.** Propose a new rule only when the evidence shows a capable agent gets it wrong without one, and make a mechanical rule a check instead of a sentence.
- **The owner decides.** Hand every proposal to the owner, and leave the skills, agents and rules as they are.

## Steps

1. Find the evidence in the window through `search-sessions`: the owner correcting an agent, a question the owner had to ask, a gate that refused correct work, a review that kept returning, and friction a session reported.
2. Group the evidence by the skill, agent or repo rule each piece traces to, and trace each group to the waste it cost and its cause, as [references/waste-and-cause.md](references/waste-and-cause.md) says.
3. For each skill with a pattern, read the current version whole and its history over the window, then judge it whole.
4. Report.

## Report

Lead with the skills that need a rewrite or a cut. For each one, give:

- its purpose in one line
- the pattern, with its citations
- the waste it cost and the cause it traces to
- why the current version causes it
- the change: a rewrite from its purpose, a cut, or, rarely, a rule or check

Then list the one-offs one line each, and what you searched for and did not find.
